# Two-pose mounting calibration

> **Status (2026-11-15): fallback.** The app's primary direction calibration is now [movement calibration](motion-calibration.md) (neutral, then a bow and knee lifts or squats, accumulated without holding a pose). This held skiing-pose method remains available in Setup as **Use held skiing pose instead** and through `TrackingCore::calibrate_forward`, unchanged. Both solve the same left heading alignment and right mounting described below.

This experiment corrects a concrete failure of the initial neutral-only implementation. A neutral pose can make all segment orientations look aligned while their subsequent bend directions remain wrong. A facing arrow helps explain the reference frame, but cannot identify a tracker mount by itself.

## Separate the two rotations

Use `q_i(t) = D_i B_i(t) M_i`: the IMU quaternion combines an independent sensor-world heading `D_i`, the body segment orientation `B_i`, and the physical sensor mount `M_i`. Gravity constrains tilt; it does not give an absolute heading. The desired body estimate has a heading correction on the **left** and the inverse physical mount on the **right**.

The original neutral implementation used `q(t) q(0)^-1 R`, where `R` is the desired reference heading. This is not generally `B(t)`. For example, a sensor-world yaw of 90 degrees can conjugate a forward bend into a side bend. An HMD supplies `R`, but a single static pose still cannot separate `D` from `M`.

Two poses supply an additional axis observation. For the same unchanged mount, `q(ski) q(neutral)^-1` cancels `M` and reveals the rotation axis in that sensor's world frame. Under the instructed sagittal movement, a signed horizontal axis determines the missing heading factor. Recompute the right-side mount using the original neutral observation and this independently solved left-side factor. Future heading recovery changes the left-side factor while preserving the learned physical mount.

## User contract and limitations

1. Stand upright, feet parallel, arms relaxed, with head and torso facing together. Capture neutral after the three-second countdown. In live mode the headset defines world forward. Desktop mode maps the chosen physical direction to the preview's fixed forward arrow.
2. Keep facing that direction. Move knees forward with feet flat, move hips back, and tilt the torso and pelvis forward into a comfortable skiing pose. Hold still for the second three-second capture. In +Y-up/-Z-forward coordinates, thighs tilt with positive X pitch; calves, chest and pelvis tilt with negative X pitch.
3. Return upright and check each knee bend and torso movement. Repeating neutral within the same session retains learned mounting directions. Clearing calibration, a tracker restart, or changing tracking mode requires the two steps again.

The procedure requires informative motion from **each** of the six core segments. A nearly upright pelvis does not reveal the hip tracker's heading, even when the knees have moved a lot. The app reports the insufficient segment rather than substituting a guessed heading. Inputs must satisfy the numerical excitation and stability checks; updates are committed together.

Those checks do not prove the user performed the instructed pose. A lateral bend, or bending a segment in the opposite direction, can be indistinguishable from a different sensor heading. A second unconstrained IMU pose cannot resolve that ambiguity. Headset heading, known mount geometry, additional guided movements, or independent positional observations can provide further evidence. Upper-arm and foot mounts need their own informative procedure; the six-sensor skiing step must not certify them.

This is a model-conditional mounting estimate, not a new absolute compass or demonstrated accuracy improvement over SlimeVR. The current implementation does not compensate arbitrary gravity-estimation errors, strap motion, or large drift between the two captures.

## Research context and follow-up

SlimeVR documents separate neutral/full resets and mounting calibration, including the skiing pose in its [quick setup guide](https://docs.slimevr.dev/quick-setup.html) and [terminology](https://docs.slimevr.dev/glossary.html). Its [July 11, 2026 development update](https://slimevr.dev/blog/weekly-update-239/) identifies mounting difficulty as a major user problem and describes work toward a step-based replacement. These are precedents and usability lessons, not evidence that our implementation is superior or that its announced replacement is present in a particular installed release.

The held-pose implementation also solves each measured orientation in the stable endpoint window and rejects heading estimates that vary by more than eight degrees from the mean solution. This prevents averaging inconsistent axis evidence into one apparently clean quaternion. It does not add independent pose evidence. The minimum useful inclination change is ten degrees, computed as `2 asin(sin(angle/2) * horizontal_axis_length)`; pure yaw cannot satisfy it. Total rotation is capped at eighty degrees. Axis elevation is limited to twenty-five degrees and sagittal-model residual to ten degrees. These two off-axis checks are related, not independent evidence of correct anatomy.

Worn feedback exposed excessive strictness in the original fifteen-degree axis/six-degree residual limits, and in sharing neutral's two-degree stability gate. Forward capture now allows up to four degrees of orientation variation under default configuration, while retaining the 0.65-second observation window, acceleration, freshness, transport and eight-degree heading-consistency checks. Neutral and recovery keep their original stability limits. The app gives the user ten seconds to settle after the same three-second countdown, with no automatic restart after timeout. These are proposed engineering tolerances, not demonstrated accuracy bounds; the looser capture requires further worn validation. The synthetic tests include modest combined yaw/pitch where acceptance carries a small nonzero heading error, rather than implying exact recovery for imperfect poses.

In the later [v22.0.0-rc.4 step implementation](https://github.com/SlimeVR/SlimeVR-Server/blob/v22.0.0-rc.4/server/core/src/main/java/dev/slimevr/stepmounting/recording-processor.kt#L49-L81), SlimeVR records headset displacement and integrates each tracker's acceleration. It estimates constant acceleration bias using the final-rest assumption, recomputes displacement, and compares horizontal displacement directions. It therefore uses each tracker's motion as evidence; simply making the HMD displacement the forward arrow would not solve every sensor's mounting. The [RC2 notes](https://github.com/SlimeVR/SlimeVR-Server/releases/tag/v22.0.0-rc.2) document acceleration offsets and firmware compatibility constraints. Our firmware 0.7.2 stream's coordinate conventions, gravity subtraction, timing and coverage must be established before this method can be trusted here.

Potential improvements worth testing are a short continuous movement rather than one held endpoint, per-segment progress indicators, repeated bend consistency, separate hip/leg motions for limited flexibility, and independent head/hand contact observations. A bend-and-return sequence can check angular closure. A headset-assisted step could be fitted on one short motion and checked on a second, with start/end stillness, adequate displacement and packet coverage required for every sensor. Those are candidate experiments, not implemented automatic steps. Body constraints can reject some implausible results, but reusing those constraints as the only accuracy metric would be circular.

## Manual fallback

The advanced editor selects an assigned tracker and distinguishes two operations. **Bend direction** makes a paired left-yaw/right-mount change that keeps the captured neutral orientation aligned while rotating the movement axes. **Physical mount trim** applies a local `Rx * Ry * Rz` quaternion on the right; X/Z edits can intentionally change neutral tilt. Its colored drag handles and numeric sliders edit real mounting parameters. Undo restores the preceding adjustment; Reset returns to the mounting used when that editing session began. Explicit per-tracker confirmation records a user assertion after movement checks, not a successful automatic solve. The guided two-pose procedure remains the primary path.
